Este documento contém os trechos atualizados conforme a Auditoria Final e a Fase 7.

## 1. Abstract / Resumo
**Results:** A total of 10 studies involving 103,417 participants were included in the meta-analysis. The AI models demonstrated a pooled sensitivity of **87.9% (95% CI: 78.6%–93.5%)** and a pooled specificity of **97.9% (95% CI: 89.7%–99.6%)**. The area under the ROC curve (AUC-SROC) was **0.955**. Heterogeneity was moderate to high (I²: 61% to 88.8% depending on the method).

## 2. Methods
**Architecture Classification:** Models were classified into three categories: Vision-Language Models (VLM) for multimodal visual processing (e.g., GPT-4V, Hong 2025a/b/c, Ostrovsky 2025), Large Language Models (LLM) for text-only reasoning (e.g., Huang 2023, 2025, Lee 2025), and Agentic Generative AI (e.g., Noh 2026).
**Statistical Analysis:** Bivariate random-effects meta-analysis (Reitsma model) was performed. Continuity correction (+0.5) was applied to studies with zero counts (Noh 2026).

## 3. Results
**Pooled Performance:** The bivariate pooling of 7 studies with raw data (N=103,417) yielded a robust AUC of 0.955. Subgroup analysis revealed that VLM architectures (N=4) achieved higher sensitivity (95.2%) compared to text-only LLMs (78.6%).

## 4. Discussion (Alertas de Viés)
The results must be interpreted considering the dominance of **Huang 2025**, which accounts for 94.4% of the participants. Additionally, the high prevalence (81%) in **Noh 2026** indicates a highly selected population, which may overestimate clinical performance. The classification of **Ostrovsky 2025** as VLM reflects its use of advanced multimodal reasoning (GPT-4), though current literature acknowledges inherent uncertainty in classifying high-level LLMs.
